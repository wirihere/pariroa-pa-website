Our Future

Pariroa Pā 2050 — He taonga tuku iho mō ngā whakatipuranga e haere ake nei. A treasure handed down for the generations still to come.

Te Mahere

What the Plan is

In 2026 the trustees adopted Pariroa Pā 2050 — our intergenerational aspirations plan. In one sentence, it commits this marae to being thriving, self-sustaining, climate-resilient and reo-rich by 2050.

The Plan is built on ten pou — the posts that hold up the work — and gathered under Te Oranga o Pariroa, our pathway to self-reliance.

It is a living plan: reviewed every five years, carried by five-year action plans, with progress reported back to whānau each year.

Who we do it for — the whānau of today and the generations to come.

Our Vision

“By 2050, Pariroa Pā Marae will be a thriving, self-sustaining, climate-resilient, reo-rich marae that continues to nurture the descendants of Ngāti Tūpito, Ngāti Ringi, Ngāti Hine, Kotuku and Tuatahi. Pariroa Pā will stand as a place where tikanga flourishes, whānau return home, mātauranga is protected, and future generations are empowered to lead.

From Pariroa Pā 2050 — our intergenerational aspirations plan.

Ngā Pou

The ten pou

The Plan keeps its own numbering — ten pou, gathered here in three whāinga.

Our people and our reo

Four pou about the people themselves — the reo, the wellbeing, the rangatahi, the knowledge.

Pou 1

Reo, tikanga and cultural revitalisation

The reo lives here, on this marae, in the mouths of our mokopuna.

  • annual reo wānanga, and kaumātua oral histories recorded while they can be
  • waiata, karakia and tuku iho held in a digital archive; a marae tikanga handbook takes shape
  • reo programmes grow for tamariki and mokopuna

Pou 2

Whānau wellbeing

A marae that cares for its own, from the cradle to the kaumātua seat.

  • whānau reunions bring the descendants home each year; hauora programmes look after body and wairua
  • support networks and a marae-based crisis response — no whānau faces trouble alone
  • leadership grown across the generations

Pou 7

Rangatahi leadership

The pā belongs to the young; they will hold it next.

  • rangatahi step onto the paepae through trustee mentoring and governance internships
  • leadership wānanga build their footing
  • a succession framework makes the pathway real, not just hoped for

Pou 8

Digital archive and knowledge protection

What is remembered is protected; what is protected is never lost.

  • photographs, whakapapa records and oral histories digitised
  • held safe in cloud and physical storage — a taonga for the generations who will look for themselves here

Our whenua and our taonga

Three pou about the place itself — the climate, the buildings and urupā, the kai.

Pou 3

Climate adaptation and resilience

A pā ready for the weather the future brings.

  • climate risks assessed, flood and storm plans made, emergency shelter capability built
  • adaptation funding, native planting and ecological restoration that heal the whenua

Pou 6

Taonga, whare and urupā protection

The houses, the carvings and the sleeping places of our tūpuna, kept with care.

  • a comprehensive asset register and long-term maintenance plan look after every whare
  • a heritage conservation programme protects the taonga
  • an urupā protection strategy keeps Te Moengaroa undisturbed

Pou 10

Mana kai — food sovereignty

The pā feeds its own, as it always has.

  • māra kai, productive gardens, orchards and food forests grow on the whenua; traditional food knowledge kept alive
  • emergency food reserves, and whānau growing and preserving kai — real mana over what we eat

Standing on our own

Three pou about independence — energy, water and the money that keeps the lights on.

Pou 4

Energy sovereignty

Power made on the pā, not bought from far away.

  • solar generation and battery storage come onto the marae
  • reliance on the external grid falls, and the pā keeps its energy through emergencies

Pou 5

Water sovereignty

Clean water, secure, from our own whenua.

  • the bore is protected and maintained, and water is tested regularly
  • storage capacity grows, and emergency water supply plans are ready before they're needed

Pou 9

Financial sustainability

A marae with its own reserves is a marae that answers to no one.

  • a marae reserve fund is built and funding sources diversified
  • financial governance kept strong, infrastructure investment secured — the kaupapa outlives any one funding round

Our Commitment

We acknowledge those who came before us, those who stand with us today, and those yet to come. We accept our responsibility as kaitiaki of Pariroa Pā Marae and commit ourselves to protecting and strengthening this place for future generations.
